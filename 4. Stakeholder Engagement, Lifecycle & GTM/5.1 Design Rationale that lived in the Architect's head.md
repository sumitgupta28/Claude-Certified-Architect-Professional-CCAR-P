### Core Architectural Pillars for CCAR-P:

* **The "Diagram-Only" Anti-Pattern:** Inheriting a thorough system architecture diagram without associated design rationale or rejected alternatives creates a single point of failure during team transitions.
* **Context Strategy & Data-Residency Interdependence:** Context management choices (e.g., prompt caching, dynamic retrieval, remote context proxies) are often bound to strict **data-residency constraints**. Altering context handling to solve a latency or throughput issue risks bypassing in-region data handling rules.
* **Root Cause of Architectural Regression:** Downstream engineering teams make optimization proposals based on visible performance metrics (e.g., latency, cost, throughput). Without explicit record of **rejected alternatives** and **decision rationales**, replacements will naturally revert to intuitive or performant designs that were previously discarded due to hidden compliance or security bounds.

## 2. Clear & Simple Explanation

1. **The Trap of "Look at the Diagram":** Imagine moving into a house where the previous owner left a blueprint showing a wall in the middle of the living room, but left no notes explaining *why*. When you knock the wall down to make space, the roof collapses because the wall was load-bearing. An architecture diagram shows *what* was built, but it cannot tell you which parts are load-bearing safety features.
2. **Why Performance Tuning Breaks Compliance:** When a new architect inherits a system, they look at performance dashboards. If they see high latency or high token consumption in the context pipeline, their instinct is to rewrite the **context strategy** to speed things up.
3. **The Postmortem Finding:** In this case, the original architect built a specific, slower context strategy specifically to keep customer data inside a local geographic region. When the replacement architect changed the strategy to fix a latency bottleneck, they accidentally sent regulated financial data across geographic borders, causing a severe compliance violation.
4. **The Fix for Handoffs:** Handoff documentation must carry the **rejected alternatives** and **decision rationales** so that future teams know *why* a seemingly sub-optimal design was chosen.

1. **Original Architecture (Week 1–12):** The original Lead Architect implements an in-region context routing strategy. System context is retrieved solely from a local regional database before passing to Claude via an in-region VPC endpoint. This design introduces a 400ms latency penalty but strictly satisfies national financial data-residency laws.
2. **The Turnover & Missing Rationale:** The original architect departs in Week 12, leaving behind an architecture diagram showing the components but omitting the rationale row explaining *why* a global edge cache was rejected.
3. **The Unknowing Violation:** The replacement architect identifies the 400ms latency bottleneck during peak usage and switches to a global context edge-caching layer. While latency drops by 60%, sensitive client financial records are now cached across non-compliant global edge nodes.
4. **The Postmortem Resolution:** The compliance breach triggers a formal postmortem. The root cause is attributed not to engineering negligence, but to incomplete handoff documentation that failed to mark the in-region context strategy as a **load-bearing compliance control**.

## Key Technical Terms

* **Architectural Postmortem:** A formal failure analysis conducted after a production incident to trace root causes back to systemic process, documentation, or design deficiencies.
* **Context Strategy:** The technical design dictating how prompt context, reference data, and conversation history are assembled, cached, retrieved, and passed to the model during inference.
* **Data-Residency Constraint:** A legal or regulatory rule mandating that data (including prompts, context payloads, and generated outputs) must remain within specific geographic or sovereign network boundaries.
* **Rejected Alternatives:** Documented architectural choices that were evaluated during design but explicitly passed over due to compliance, security, or technical constraints.
* **Architectural Rationale:** The documented "why" behind a design decision, detailing the trade-offs, constraints, and business requirements that forced a specific implementation choices over others.
* **Load-Bearing Compliance Control:** An architectural component or data-handling pattern specifically engineered to satisfy legal or security constraints, which cannot be removed or altered without breaching compliance.
